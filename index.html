"To go wrong in one's own way is better than to go right in someone else's."

I have an ardent desire to create, one that extends across any domain that captures my interest. I'm in pursuit of a life filled with meaning, knowing that the pursuit itself may never truly end. And maybe that's the point: the pursuit is the meaning. I try not to lose sight of the beauty in small things, even in the mundane phases of this journey, and I pause from time to time to take it all in.

Electrical Engineering student at Texas A&M and researcher at the Texas A&M Transportation Institute. Currently focused on RF/microwave systems, V2X communication, computer networking, and embedded electronics.

Philosophy

Beliefs and mental models, updated whenever I change my mind. Click any item to read more.

  • i.
    Creation is the highest form of meaning.
    Creation is broader than I once thought. It doesn't require paint, code, or instruments. Cooking a meal is creation. A well-crafted argument is creation. Structuring your day with intention is creation. Arranging a playlist for a specific mood is creation. What makes an act creative is not the medium but the intention, bringing something into being that didn't exist before, even if only in form or arrangement.
    +
  • ii.
    Everything is impermanent.
    This isn't pessimism, it's the most liberating fact I know. The difficult phase ends. So does the easy one. Clinging to either is the source of most suffering. I return to this when I'm too attached to an outcome, or too afraid to let something go. Impermanence is not loss. It's just time doing what time does.
    +
  • iii.
    You are not your thoughts.
    Thoughts arise automatically, unbidden, sometimes ugly, often irrational. You didn't choose them, so why identify with them? Noticing a thought without becoming it is the whole practice of meditation condensed into one sentence. This one took me a long time to actually feel rather than just intellectually understand. There's a gap between the thought and the self, and that gap is where freedom lives.
    +
  • iv.
    Create for its own sake, work for its own sake.
    The outcome is a byproduct of the love, never the other way around.
    +
  • v.
    Don't let the figs rot and fall around you. Pick one, stay patient, and finish it, you'll find more that have ripened within reach.
    The fear of picking the "wrong" path, and thereby foreclosing all the other paths your imagination can generate, is paralyzing and universal. The fig tree metaphor (from Sylvia Plath's The Bell Jar) captures something real: an infinite field of ripening choices, and you, starving, unable to choose any of them. But the answer isn't to choose better. It's to choose at all, and trust that choosing teaches you more than deliberating ever will. Action reveals the next action. Inaction reveals nothing. And the figs you didn't pick? They weren't yours to begin with.
    +
  • v.
    Instead of assuming "this is how things are", think "this is how things appear to me, given the machinery I currently have."
    Your brain rarely presents raw reality. One of the deeper realizations of intellectual growth is that our experience of the world is heavily filtered through biology, culture, memory, language, and attention. Need more proof? Watch the McGurk Effect demonstration.
    +

Phasor Lab ↗
An interactive visualization of rotating complex vectors, Euler's formula, and their relationship to AC sinusoidal signals. Adjust the controls and watch everything update in real time. Created during ECEN 214 because I wanted to better visualize phasors.
Completed: 4/18/26
V2X Sandbox ↗
V2X Sandbox lets you build a connected intersection piece by piece and watch the invisible traffic of V2X messages move between cars, roadside units, sensors, and the cloud. It's a drag-and-drop simulator that also walks through animated real-world scenarios, visualizes the interior of the hardware, and quizzes you on it, all in one offline web page. I made it during my summer research internship at the Texas A&M Transportation Institute (TTI), because V2X has so many moving parts you can't see, and as a spatial learner, building an interactive visualizer is the way I learn best.
Last Update: 8/23/26

Lecture Hall ↗
I built LectureHall to turn YouTube playlists into real courses: chapters, timestamped notes, quizzes, and progress you can actually track; and to catalogue the professors teaching them in one searchable directory. The whole thing started as a workaround: I have social media blocked, so embeds were the only way I could reach lectures at all. It turned out the stripped-down version was the better one: no sidebar, no autoplay, no algorithm pulling you somewhere else.
